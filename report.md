Executive Report: E-commerce Performance Analysis

Revenue Trends:
Over the past 12 months, the business showed strong growth during 2025. Revenue increased steadily from around 217K in April 2025 to more than 1.7M in December 2025. Several months had growth rates between 30% and 50%, especially towards the end of the year.

In January 2026, there was a sharp drop in revenue of about 57%, and the number of orders dropped by a similar percentage. This suggests that something significant happened during that period rather than a normal fluctuation.

After that, the business recovered quickly. Revenue increased again in February and March 2026, with strong growth rates, which indicates that the issue in January was temporary.

The main driver of growth was the increase in the number of customers and orders. The average order value stayed relatively stable, so customers were not spending much more per order. Instead, the business grew because more customers were making purchases.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Customer Retention:
Customer retention improved over time. Early cohorts in 2025 had lower retention, with around 35% to 48% of customers making a repeat purchase within 30 days.

Later cohorts showed much stronger retention, with some reaching over 80% or even 90%. This suggests that the company improved in keeping customers engaged after their first purchase.

However, the most recent cohorts may look stronger than they really are, since there has not been enough time to fully observe their long-term behavior.

Overall, the trend indicates that retention became a strength for the business over time.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Category Performance:
The Electronics category is the main contributor to revenue. It consistently makes up around 50% to 60% of total sales and its share increased over time.

Other categories such as Clothing, Home & Kitchen, and Sports contribute around 10% to 15% each, while Health & Beauty and Books contribute much less.

This means the business depends heavily on one category. While Electronics is performing well, this creates a risk if demand in that category decreases.

At the same time, there may be opportunities to grow the smaller categories and reduce this dependency.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendations:
First, the company should investigate what caused the drop in January 2026, since it affected all segments and seems related to customer acquisition.

Second, since growth is mainly driven by new customers, the company should continue investing in acquisition strategies that worked well during late 2025.

Third, the company should maintain its current retention efforts, since retention has improved significantly and is now an important strength.

Fourth, it would be useful to try increasing average order value through methods like bundling or cross-selling, since AOV has remained mostly unchanged.

Finally, the business should work on growing other categories to reduce reliance on Electronics and make revenue more balanced.
